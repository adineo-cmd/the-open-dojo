Training syllabi

What do you actually learn, in what order? Five representative curricula — a taekwondo kup ladder, a BJJ white-to-blue checklist, a judo kyu ladder, and the unpinned progressions of Wing Chun and Muay Thai. Every school sequences these differently: treat these as maps of the territory, not the territory.

WT taekwondo, kup by kup

A representative Kukkiwon-style ladder from white belt to the doorstep of black. Forms per grade come straight from the Taegeuk table; the rest is the usual dojang flavor.

GradeBeltFormNew material (typical)
10th kupWhite—Etiquette, charyeot & gyeongnye, walking stance, arae makki, ap chagi
9th kupWhite / yellow stripe—Momtong makki, olgul makki, basic punch series
8th kupYellowTaegeuk Il JangFront-snap combinations, ap seogi walking turns
7th kupYellow / green stripeTaegeuk I JangYop chagi (side kick), twin forearm block
6th kupGreenTaegeuk Sam JangDollyo chagi, first one-step sparring
5th kupGreen / blue stripeTaegeuk Sa JangNaeryeo chagi (axe kick), hammer-fist series
4th kupBlueTaegeuk O JangKick combinations, elbow strikes, free sparring begins
3rd kupBlue / red stripeTaegeuk Yuk JangDwit chagi (back kick), continuous step sparring
2nd kupRedTaegeuk Chil JangTiger-stance work, breaking (kyok-pa), sparring emphasis
1st kupRed / black stripeTaegeuk Pal JangFull curriculum review, advanced breaking, competition rounds
1st danBlackKoryoThe beginning — see what black belt means

BJJ: the white-to-blue checklist

No exam — just a body of skill most academies expect before blue. If you can do this list against a resisting partner of your size, you're in blue territory:

Positions & escapes

  • Closed guard: maintain, open, and attack
  • Mount and side control: survive, then escape (elbow-knee escape)
  • Back control: escape and defend the choke
  • Half guard: recover full guard
  • Turtle: roll or re-guard safely

Offense & takedowns

  • Guard passes: knee-cut and toreando at minimum
  • Submissions from top: armbar, americana, cross-collar, guillotine
  • Submissions from guard: triangle, armbar, kimura, cross-collar
  • Two clean sweeps from closed guard
  • One double-leg and one single-leg takedown
  • Technical stand-up, every time, under pressure

Stripes along the way usually mark these milestones as they land. Time varies wildly — 12 to 30 months is honest.

Wing Chun: milestones without belts

Many Wing Chun schools grade sparsely — progress shows in what you can do, in this order:

StageMilestone
1The stance, the centerline, and Siu Nim Tau — performed from memory, relaxed
2Single-arm chi sau with correct tan, bong, and fok sau shapes
3Chum Kiu — turning, stepping, and chasing the center while moving
4Cross-arm and double-arm chi sau; lap sau and dan chi drills flow
5Biu Jee and recovery principles — the emergency toolkit
6Mook yan jong (wooden dummy), then the long pole and knives

Muay Thai: the unbelted ladder

Thai gyms traditionally reward progress with respect and ring records, not belts. The milestones beginners cross, roughly in order:

StageMilestone
1Stance, bounce, and turn — plus a teep and jab that land where aimed
2A roundhouse from both sides with the shin, on pads, without winding up
3Clinch comfortably: plum control, pummeling, knees from the tie-up
4Three-round pad sets at fight pace; elbows and catch-and-return basics
5Light technical sparring — defense holds, gas tank holds longer
6First interclub or smoker — the traditional first test

Maps, not laws. Real curricula differ between every federation, association, and teacher — sometimes on purpose. Bring this page to your instructor and ask what your school's order is; the asking itself teaches you how they think. Pair it with Belts & ranking for how the promotion side works.
