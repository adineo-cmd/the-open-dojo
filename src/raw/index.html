What this library is for

Written for two readers in particular — though everyone is welcome.

New to martial arts?

Choosing a school is easier when you can tell a roundhouse from a teep and Judo from Jiu-Jitsu. Start with the featured styles, compare what each art trains, then use the school checklist before you visit a dojo. A little vocabulary goes a long way in a first class, so skim the terminology section on the way.

Already training?

Class time is precious; the details are easy to forget. Use these pages to review your kata the night before a belt test, double-check the Japanese or Korean count, study how a throw actually works, and fill the gaps a twice-a-week schedule leaves with home conditioning.

This is a wiki at heart. Martial knowledge has always been handed from teacher to student; the library just writes it down. If you spot an error or know something these pages don't, the reference should grow from your corrections too — send notes to the editors and they'll be folded in.

Build a small home dojo

You don't need a gym. A cleared patch of floor and a few pieces of gear carry most of the load between classes.

Practice mats

Interlocking foam tiles protect joints on throws, breakfalls, and ground work. Even a 2 × 2-meter square is enough for solo drills.

Striking bag

A hanging or freestanding heavy bag builds power and cardio; a wall-mounted pad or makiwara hones accuracy on straight punches.

Focus mitts

Cheap, quiet, and brilliantly effective — a partner with mitts turns combinations into reactions instead of choreography.

Kicking shield

Absorbs full-power roundhouses and knee strikes that no bag or partner should take bare. Holds up for years.

Mirror

The oldest training tool in the book. Check your stance depth, guard height, and kata timing against what you feel.

Before you try anything here

These pages describe techniques; they cannot watch your partner's elbow or catch your fall. Books and websites inform training — they don't replace a qualified instructor. Practice new techniques only under supervision, at an agreed pace, on appropriate surfaces. Train safe, and the library will still be here tomorrow.
