空手道
Okinawa → Japan

Karate

“Empty hand” — an Okinawan art of Chinese descent, organized in the 20th century into the great schools (ryū) that now span the world.

FactDetail
OriginOkinawa (Ryukyu Kingdom), formalized in mainland Japan from the 1920s
Key foundersGichin Funakoshi (Shotokan), Chojun Miyagi (Goju-Ryu), Kenwa Mabuni (Shito-Ryu), Mas Oyama (Kyokushin)
Core trainingKihon (basics) · Kata (forms) · Kumite (sparring) — the three-legged stool
Sparring formatPoint-stop, continuous semi-contact, or full knockdown, depending on school
Road to black belt~3–5 years of steady attendance

A typical class

  1. Warm-up and joint mobility (10–15 min)
  2. Kihon — lines of blocks, punches, kicks (20 min)
  3. Kata — your rank's form, corrected in detail (15 min)
  4. Kumite or partner drills (15 min)
  5. Cool-down, kneel, mokuso, bow out

Known for & famous names

Deep stances, exacting basics, kata culture, and the strongest belt ceremony tradition. Gichin Funakoshi brought it to Japan; Mas Oyama proved it in full contact; Lyoto Machida carried its timing into MMA championship gold.

Start here if: you want discipline, structure, and a solo-practiceable art. See the kata tables and style notes.

跆拳道
Korea

Taekwondo

“The way of the foot and fist” — Korea's global art, built on the fastest kicking library in the martial world and one of only two Asian fighting arts in the Olympic Games.

FactDetail
OriginKorea, consolidated in the 1940s–50s from kwans with karate lineage and older Korean kicking traditions; name adopted 1955
Two familiesKukkiwon/WT (Olympic) and ITF (General Choi Hong-hi's line) — different forms, different sparring
Core trainingKicking drills · Poomsae/teul (forms) · Step-sparring · Free sparring
Sparring formatOlympic: electronic-scored kicks to body and head; ITF: continuous with punches allowed
Road to black belt~2.5–4 years

A typical class

  1. Warm-up, flexibility (15 min)
  2. Basic kicks and stances down the floor (15 min)
  3. Poomsae or teul of your rank (15 min)
  4. Target kicks and step-sparring (10 min)
  5. Free sparring for color belts and up

Known for & famous names

Head-height and spinning kicks, Olympic pedigree since Sydney 2000, and the most international student body of any art. Choi Hong-hi codified the ITF; champions like Steven Lopez and Jade Jones defined the Olympic era.

Start here if: you love dynamic kicking and clear, measurable progress. See the poomsae tables.

柔道
Japan · 1882

Judo

Jigoro Kano's “gentle way” — the art that invented the modern belt system, joined the Olympics first, and taught the world that balance beats strength.

FactDetail
OriginJapan, founded by Jigoro Kano in 1882 (the Kodokan), from classical jujutsu
Core trainingUchi-komi (repetition fitting) · Nage-waza (throws) · Newaza (groundwork) · Randori (free practice)
Sparring formatShiai — one clean ippon (throw, pin, or submission) ends the match
OlympicSince Tokyo 1964; women since Barcelona 1992
Road to black belt~3–6 years; some federations credit competition results toward promotion

A typical class

  1. Warm-up and ukemi (breakfalls) (15 min)
  2. Uchi-komi — throw entries repeated (15 min)
  3. New technique of the day, practiced in pairs (15 min)
  4. Randori — free sparring, standing and ground (15 min)

Known for & famous names

Spectacular throws and relentless grip fighting. Teddy Riner dominated heavyweight judo for a decade; Ronda Rousey took its armbar from Olympic medal to MMA title; Keiko Fukuda, Kano's own student, became the first woman 10th dan.

Start here if: you want live competition, real falls, and the oldest international infrastructure. See the throws library.

Jiu-Jitsu
Brazil · early 1900s

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

The ground-fighting chess game: control first, submit second, strength optional. BJJ rewrote what a smaller person can do to a larger one.

FactDetail
OriginBrazil, early 1900s — judo ground work via Mitsuyo Maeda, developed by Carlos and Hélio Gracie and their students
Core trainingPositional drills · Techniques of the day · Live rolling — every class ends on the mat
Sparring formatRolling (cooperative to competitive); tournaments score positions and end on submission
Belt ladderWhite → blue → purple → brown → black; typically ~8–12 years to black
UniformsGi (collar grips) and no-gi (athletic wear) — most schools train both

A typical class

  1. Warm-up with solo movement drills (10 min)
  2. Technique of the day, drilled in pairs (25 min)
  3. Positional sparring (specific start points) (10 min)
  4. Free rolling at agreed intensity (15 min)

Known for & famous names

The guard, the submission game, and the culture of live resistance. Hélio Gracie proved the leverage thesis; Rickson Gracie became its legend; Marcelo Garcia and Marcus “Buchecha” Almeida its modern artists.

Start here if: you want hard, honest training with a gentler learning curve on the body than judo. See positions and submissions.

มวยไทย
Thailand

Muay Thai

“The art of eight limbs” — Thailand's national sport, where fists, elbows, knees, and shins all score, and the clinch is a fighting range of its own.

FactDetail
OriginThailand, from centuries of battlefield and ring tradition; codified with modern stadium rules
Core trainingShadowboxing · Pad work (the heart of every class) · Bag work · Clinching · Sparring
Sparring formatFive three-minute rounds in Thai rules; light technical sparring is the gym norm
Signature weaponsThe shin roundhouse, the teep, and the plum-position knees
RanksTradition uses no belts — ringside records and Mongkhon headbands carry the honor

A typical class

  1. Skip rope and shadowbox (15 min)
  2. Pad rounds with a partner or trainer (30 min)
  3. Clinch and knee drills (10 min)
  4. Bag work, then optional light sparring

Known for & famous names

The most complete striking ruleset short of MMA. Legends run from Nai Khanom Tom (the folklore founding hero) to Samart Payakaroon, the craft of Saenchai, and modern stadium stars like Rodtang Jitmuangnon.

Start here if: you want fight-proven striking and a strong aerobic engine. See the kicks and clinch sections.

詠春
China (Guangdong)

Wing Chun

A close-range Southern Chinese system of economy and structure — straight lines, simultaneous attack and defense, and arms trained to read pressure.

FactDetail
OriginGuangdong, China; legend credits a woman, Yim Wing-chun, for the style's principles
Three empty-hand formsSiu Nim Tau (“little idea”) · Chum Kiu (“seeking the bridge”) · Biu Jee (“darting fingers”)
Signature drillChi sau — “sticky hands,” reading force through contact
EquipmentThe mook yan jong — the wooden dummy — plus long pole (luk dim boon gwun) and knives in senior grades
Famous lineageIp Man, who taught in Hong Kong from the 1950s — and whose students carried it worldwide

A typical class

  1. Stance work and the first form (15 min)
  2. Punching chains and turning drills (15 min)
  3. Chi sau in pairs — single arm, then double (20 min)
  4. Applications and wooden-dummy work for seniors

Known for & famous names

The centerline theory, chain punching, and the most famous lineage story in kung fu: Ip Man — and his student Bruce Lee, who took the principles and built Jeet Kune Do.

Start here if: you prefer close range, sensitivity, and theory you can chew on for years.

合気道
Japan · early 1900s

Aikido

Morihei Ueshiba's art of resolving conflict without destroying the attacker — blending, joint locks, and pins, taught through flowing partnered forms.

FactDetail
OriginJapan, founded by Morihei Ueshiba (ōsensei) from Daito-ryu aiki-jujutsu and his own spiritual study
Core trainingPaired technique (nage/uke roles) · Ukemi · Weapons forms with wooden sword and staff
SparringNone competitive — randori (multiple-attacker flow) and testing forms instead
RanksKyu and dan; white belt to black with little in between, in many dojos
VariantsYoshinkan (structured, taught to Tokyo police) and Shodokan/Tomiki (the competitive line)

A typical class

  1. Weapons or empty-hand warm-up (15 min)
  2. Ukemi practice — falling is the first skill (10 min)
  3. Paired techniques of the day, both roles (25 min)
  4. Weapons kata or multiple-attacker flow for seniors

Known for & famous names

The most philosophy-forward of the Japanese arts — and, through Steven Seagal's films, its most recognizable popularizer. Serious critics and defenders agree on one thing: the ukemi training is world-class.

Start here if: you want flowing, cooperative training and a long philosophical path; pair it with a live art if self-defense is the goal.

קרב מגע
Israel · 1940s onward

Krav Maga

“Contact combat” — the self-defense system built for the worst hour of your life, borrowed from every art that worked and stripped to what survives stress.

FactDetail
OriginDeveloped by Imi Lichtenfeld, a champion boxer and wrestler from Bratislava, for Israel's armed forces; opened to civilians in the 1960s–70s
Core trainingCombatives (striking) · Escapes from holds · Scenario drills under fatigue
PhilosophySimultaneous defense and attack, targeting soft spots, escaping — never “winning a fight”
GradingPractitioner/graduate/expert levels tested at seminars and camps
RulebookNone by design — but gyms still drill with control, padding, and agreed intensity

A typical class

  1. Warm-up with combatives (15 min)
  2. Technique of the day — e.g., choke escapes (20 min)
  3. Stress drills: eyes closed, moving, exhausted (15 min)
  4. Cool-down and scenario debrief

Known for & famous names

Aggressive simplicity and scenario realism. Founder Imi Lichtenfeld is the towering figure; the system's military pedigree is its marketing and its engine. Quality varies by school — find one that pressure-tests (see what training helps).

Start here if: your goal is practical self-defense on a two-nights-a-week budget.

唐手道
Korea

Tang Soo Do

Traditional Korean karate of the Moo Duk Kwan — the pre-sport Korean art that kept its Okinawan forms, rigid discipline, and balance of hand and foot.

FactDetail
OriginKorea — Hwang Kee founded the Moo Duk Kwan in 1945; the art predates and parallels taekwondo's sport unification
Core trainingBasics · Hyung (forms — the Okinawan classics under old names) · One-step sparring · Free sparring
Forms lineageKi Cho, Pyung Ahn, Bassai, Kong Sang Koon — see the hyung table
Sparring formatSemi-contact, points-style, with emphasized control
Famous namesHwang Kee (founder) and Chuck Norris, whose tournament fame carried the art West

A typical class

  1. Formal opening with Korean commands (5 min)
  2. Basics and stances (20 min)
  3. Hyung of your rank (15 min)
  4. One-steps and controlled sparring (15 min)

Known for

Traditional etiquette, deep stances, and a syllabus frozen in the best sense — a 1930s Korean dojo experience preserved with pride.

Start here if: you want taekwondo's kicking with a traditionalist's structure.

Capoeira
Brazil · by enslaved Africans

Capoeira

An Afro-Brazilian art disguised as dance — or dance disguised as fighting; history made it both, and the roda (circle) is where the two meet.

FactDetail
OriginBrazil, created by enslaved Africans — with roots traced to the Engolo tradition of Angola
The rodaPlayers trade kicks, sweeps, and acrobatics inside a circle of musicians — a conversation, not a brawl
MusicBerimbau, atabaque, pandeiro; songs in Portuguese carry the art's history
Two main branchesCapoeira Regional (Mestre Bimba, 1930s — fighting-forward) and Capoeira Angola (Mestre Pastinha — traditional and low to the ground)
RanksCordas (cords) in colored graduation systems, varying by group

A typical class

  1. Warm-up and ginga — the defining sway-step (15 min)
  2. Esquiva (evasions), kicks, and floor movements (20 min)
  3. Sequenced partner play (15 min)
  4. Roda — everyone plays, everyone sings (10 min)

Known for

Balance and mobility few arts match, music as curriculum, and a history of resistance carried in a game. Mestre Bimba won capoeira legal recognition; Mestre Pastinha kept Angola's flame.

Start here if: you want rhythm, community, and the most fun a warm-up has ever been.

Boxing
Ancient world → modern England

Boxing

The sweet science — fists, footwork, and feints refined over three thousand years, from ancient Greek rings to the prizefighting codes of London.

FactDetail
OriginAncient Egypt and Greece (Olympic by 688 BC); modern rules from Jack Broughton's 1743 code and the Queensberry Rules (1867)
Core trainingFootwork · Shadowboxing · Bag work · Pad rounds · Sparring — the original striking curriculum
Sparring format3-minute rounds; amateur 3 rounds, professional up to 12; above the belt, hands only
OlympicSince 1904 (modern era); women since London 2012
RanksNo belts — records and title belts (world championships) carry the honor

A typical class

  1. Skip rope and joint warm-up (10 min)
  2. Footwork and shadowboxing drills (15 min)
  3. Heavy bag rounds (15 min)
  4. Pad rounds with a partner (15 min)
  5. Technical sparring for the experienced

Known for & famous names

The deepest striking meta ever built: slips, rolls, feints, and the two-handed game every combat sport borrows. Sugar Ray Robinson is the pound-for-pound benchmark; Muhammad Ali its poet; Willie Pep its defensive wizard.

Start here if: you want world-class striking fundamentals fast, and the hardest cardio in the building.

剣道
Japan

Kendo

“The way of the sword” — kenjutsu distilled into a modern discipline of bamboo swords, armor, and ferocious etiquette, where a single clean strike ends everything.

FactDetail
OriginJapan, from classical kenjutsu; modern form standardized in the 20th century under the All Japan Kendo Federation
EquipmentShinai (bamboo sword), bōgu armor (men, kote, dō, tare), hakama and keikogi
Core trainingSuburi (cutting repetitions) · Kirikaeshi (flow drill) · Waza practice · Jigeiko (free practice)
ScoringOne valid strike to men, kote, dō, or tsuki — with spirit, posture, and zanshin
RanksKyū and dan — and no visible belts at all

A typical class

  1. Bow-in, mokuso, warm-up with suburi (15 min)
  2. Kirikaeshi — the great conditioning drill (10 min)
  3. Technique practice: strikes and entries (20 min)
  4. Jigeiko — free sparring (15 min)

Known for

The loudest training halls on earth — the kiai is constant — and a scoring philosophy where a strike without spirit is not a strike. Its kata set (ten Nihon Kendo Kata forms) preserves the old sword schools intact.

Start here if: you want a lifelong discipline with deep etiquette and genuine intensity; expect a long apprenticeship before free sparring.

САМБО
Soviet Union · 1920s–30s

Sambo

The Soviet synthesis — judo's throws and native folk wrestling merged into one jacket system, with the most liberal leglock game in sport grappling.

FactDetail
OriginUSSR, developed between the wars by Viktor Spiridonov and Vasili Oshchepkov (a judo black belt under Kano)
Two main sportsSport sambo (jacket wrestling with leglocks, no chokes) and combat sambo (adds strikes, chokes, and weapons defense)
UniformKurtka jacket, shorts, and sambovki shoes — quick grips, fast scrambles
Sparring formatPoints for throws and holds; submissions end the match
Famous namesFedor Emelianenko, the heavyweight king of early MMA, and Khabib Nurmagomedov both rose from sambo mats

A typical class

  1. Gymnastic warm-up (10 min)
  2. Grip fighting and throw drills (20 min)
  3. Leglock and ground technique (15 min)
  4. Live wrestling rounds (15 min)

Known for

Explosive jacket throws and the deepest leglock arsenal outside modern no-gi grappling — the bridge between judo and MMA.

Start here if: you want judo's intensity with fewer rules about the legs.

Wrestling
The oldest sport on earth

Wrestling

Takedowns, control, and relentless pressure — wrestling appears in every culture that ever held a festival, and it anchors the takedown game of modern MMA.

FactDetail
OriginEverywhere; codified for the ancient Greek Olympics by 708 BC and present at the first modern Games in 1896
Three main codesFreestyle (international), Greco-Roman (no attacks below the waist), and folkstyle (US schools — emphasis on rides and escapes)
Core trainingStance and motion · Level changes and shots · Takedowns and defense · Rides, turns, and escapes · Conditioning, famously brutal
Sparring formatLive matches scored on takedowns, turns, escapes — pin ends all
Famous namesDan Gable (the sport's benchmark coach), Aleksandr Karelin (“the Experiment”), Cael Sanderson

A typical class

  1. Warm-up and partner movement drills (10 min)
  2. Stance, shots, and sprawls — thousands of reps (20 min)
  3. Technique of the day: takedown or turn (15 min)
  4. Live matches, short and hard (15 min)

Known for

The strongest takedown base in the world and a work ethic that defines the word. No art pressures a person for longer.

Start here if: you want the purest test of athletic will — usually via school or club programs.

Savate
France

Savate

French kickboxing in shoes — the elegant, point-scoring “boxe française” where footwork is a science and boots are legal weapons.

FactDetail
OriginFrance, 19th century — sailor's “chausson” kicking formalized by Joseph and Charles Charlemont into a ring sport
Core trainingFootwork and line drills · Punch-kick combinations · Assaut (controlled technical sparring) · Combat (full contact)
Signature kicksFouetté (whip kick with the shoe toe), chassé (side thrust), revers (hooking sole)
RanksColored gloves — blue, green, red, white, yellow, silver
RelatedCanne de combat, its stick-fencing sibling

A typical class

  1. Joint warm-up and footwork patterns (15 min)
  2. Kick technique in lines (20 min)
  3. Punch-kick combinations on pads (15 min)
  4. Assaut sparring — controlled, technical, scoreable

Known for

The only kicking art that strikes with shoes — precision over power — and the most courtly sporting culture in kickboxing.

Start here if: you love movement, craft, and a dash of Parisian style.

Fechtkunst
Europe · revived 1990s–2000s

HEMA

Historical European martial arts — the fight books of the old masters read with modern eyes, tested with nylon and steel, and fought in full protective gear.

FactDetail
OriginRebuilt from surviving manuals — Fiore's 1410 Italian treatise, the Liechtenauer German tradition, Meyer's 1570 rapier and longsword
Main weaponsLongsword, sword & buckler, rapier, sabre, polearms
Core trainingInterpreting the source · Solo drills · Structured sparring · Tournament testing
Sparring formatExchange-based bouts with judges, in masks, jackets, and gloves — full intensity, safe gear
CommunityNo central federation; clubs and international tournaments (Swordfish, Longpoint) set the standard

A typical class

  1. Footwork and cutting mechanics (15 min)
  2. Drills from the manual of the month (20 min)
  3. Structured sparring — one technique at a time (15 min)
  4. Free exchanges at full gear

Known for

Scholarship as training method: you read the source, argue the interpretation, then test it against a resisting partner in steel. The most intellectual fighting community going.

Start here if: history is your doorway — and bring the fencing mask.

散打
China

Sanda

Chinese kickboxing with a catch-and-throw clause — punches and kicks like kickboxing, plus the sweeps and throws that make a raised platform dangerous.

FactDetail
OriginChina, 20th century — traditional methods organized into a modern fighting sport
Core trainingStriking combinations · Kick-catching and throws · Takedown defense · Platform tactics
Sparring formatRounds on the leitai (raised platform); points for clean strikes — and for throwing the opponent off
GearGloves, shinguards, chest protector, headgear in amateur rules
Famous namesCung Le, who carried sanda's highlight throws into American MMA championships

A typical class

  1. Warm-up and line drills (10 min)
  2. Kick and punch combinations (20 min)
  3. Catch-and-throw work (15 min)
  4. Pad rounds and platform sparring

Known for

The most rule-clever kickboxing: when the opponent kicks, sanda catches it and rearranges their relationship with the floor.

Start here if: you want kickboxing with throws — a genuine middle path between striking and grappling.

합기도
Korea · mid-1900s

Hapkido

“The way of coordinated power” — Korea's hybrid of joint locks, throws, kicks, and weapons work, descended from Daito-ryu through Choi Yong-sul.

FactDetail
OriginKorea after WWII — Choi Yong-sul returned from Japan with Daito-ryu aiki-jujutsu; students like Ji Han-jae added Korean kicks and weapons
Core trainingJoint locks and throws · Kicking syllabus · Falling · Weapons: cane, short and long staff, rope
PrinciplesNon-resistance, circular motion, the water principle — redirect, don't oppose
Sparring formatStep-sparring and controlled drills rather than sport competition
Famous namesChoi Yong-sul (founder) and Ji Han-jae — who also fought Bruce Lee on film in Game of Death

A typical class

  1. Warm-up and falling practice (10 min)
  2. Kicking drills (15 min)
  3. Locks and throws of the day, both roles (20 min)
  4. Weapons work for senior grades

Known for

The widest technique syllabus in Korea — an encyclopedic art for students who love options.

Start here if: you want locks and weapons alongside your kicks.

കളരിപ്പയറ്റ്
Kerala, India

Kalaripayattu

Often counted among the world's oldest living martial arts — a Kerala tradition of body preparation, oil massage, empty-hand method, and a weapons ladder that ends at the flexible sword.

FactDetail
OriginKerala, South India; trained in the kalari (training ground), with roots claimed well over a millennium back
Core trainingMeippayattu (body-conditioning sequences) · Empty-hand techniques · Weapons in stages: staff, dagger, sword & shield…
Signature weaponThe urumi — a flexible ribbon of steel, arguably the most dangerous thing to learn and the last thing taught
Companion artsChavitti uzhichil — the foot-massage healing tradition of the kalari's gurukkal
CultureTraining opens with salutations to the kalari's deities; lineage and ritual run through everything

A typical class

  1. Salutations and oiling (5 min)
  2. Meippayattu — the flowing conditioning forms (20 min)
  3. Empty-hand or weapon work of your stage (20 min)
  4. Set applications and Controlled exchanges

Known for

Animal-named postures of astonishing flexibility, the deepest weapons ladder anywhere, and treatment arts trained alongside fighting.

Start here if: you want history you can feel in your hamstrings — and patience for ritual.

相撲
Japan

Sumo

Japan's national sport and a living ritual — fifteen centuries of Shinto ceremony wrapped around the simplest of questions: who moves whom?

FactDetail
OriginJapan, with ritual roots over 1,500 years old; professional structure codified under the Japan Sumo Association
The matchFrom the tachi-ai charge to a win by yorikiri (force out), oshidashi (push out), or any of 82 recognized kimarite
TrainingShiko (leg stomps), teppo (palm strikes against a pole), butsukari-geiko (charging practice), and enormous chanko meals
CalendarSix grand tournaments (basho) a year, fifteen days each
Famous namesTaiho, Chiyonofuji — and Hakuho, whose 45 top-division titles set the all-time record

A typical day

  1. Morning practice before sunrise, guests bowing to the ring
  2. Shiko and teppo repetitions — hundreds
  3. Butsukari charging drills against seniors
  4. Practice bouts, then the first meal of the day

Known for

The fastest fights in sport — most bouts end in seconds — and the richest technique taxonomy: every finishing move has a name, and every name is printed in the next day's paper.

Start here if: you are, realistically, in Japan and invited — but watch a basho; every technique in its taxonomy is a lesson in leverage.

太極拳
China · Chenjiagou

Tai Chi

The world's most practiced martial art — an internal system of slow, exact forms, push-hands sensitivity, and a health record now studied by medicine itself.

FactDetail
OriginChen village (Chenjiagou), Henan — attributed to Chen Wangting in the 17th century
The five familiesChen, Yang, Wu (Hao), Wu, and Sun — Yang Luchan carried the art from Chen village to Beijing
Core trainingSolo forms (the Yang 108; the 24-form of 1956) · Push hands (tui shou) · Weapons: straight sword, saber
Sparring formatPush hands — rooted, cooperative-to-competitive balance contests
Famous namesChen Wangting, Yang Luchan (“Yang the Invincible”), Cheng Man-ching, who shortened the form for the West

A typical class

  1. Qigong warm-up and standing meditation (15 min)
  2. Form section of the day, corrected in detail (25 min)
  3. Push hands with a partner (15 min)
  4. Weapons for senior students

Known for

Balance, joint health, and stress relief with the strongest research file of any martial art — and, underneath it, a genuine fighting method built on structure and timing.

Start here if: you want a joint-friendly, lifelong practice; speed comes later, if ever.

居合道
Japan

Iaido

The art of drawing the sword — solo kata performed against imagined opponents, where the draw, the cut, and the resheath are one continuous act of attention.

FactDetail
OriginJapan — classical lines such as Muso Jikiden Eishin-ryu and Muso Shinden-ryu, standardized for modern grading by the 12-form ZNKR set
EquipmentAn unsharpened alloy sword (iaitō) for practice; live blades are earned over years
Core trainingEtiquette and sword handling · Solo kata — seated and standing · No sparring at all
GradingKata performance before examiners, kyū and dan
Famous namesNakayama Hakudo, whose 20th-century teaching shaped both major modern lines

A typical class

  1. Bow-in, sword etiquette and handling (15 min)
  2. Kihon — draw, cut, chiburi, resheath (15 min)
  3. Seitei kata, one correction at a time (25 min)
  4. Cool, quiet bow-out

Known for

The most inward of the sword arts: four people can train in a room and hear a pin drop. Progress is measured in millimeters and years.

Start here if: you want precision and stillness — and can afford an iaitō.

摔跤
China

Shuai Jiao

Chinese jacket wrestling — arguably the oldest continuously practiced wrestling tradition on earth, fought with short-sleeved jackets and lightning belt grips.

FactDetail
OriginChina — wrestling displays date back millennia; the modern name and rules matured in the Qing era
Core trainingGrip fighting on the jacket · Throw entries and setups · Footwork · Live rounds
Sparring formatPoints for clean throws; hitting the ground first loses the exchange
Style familiesBeijing, Tianjin, Baoding and Mongolian-influenced lines, each with favorite grips
Famous namesChang Tung-sheng, “the Flying Butterfly,” who threw his way through mid-century China and Taiwan

A typical class

  1. Warm-up and footwork circles (10 min)
  2. Grip fighting and setup drills (20 min)
  3. Throw of the day, both roles (15 min)
  4. Live jacket wrestling (15 min)

Known for

Explosive lapel-and-belt throws at a pace judoka respect immediately — the bridge between China's ancient wrestling and modern jacket styles.

Start here if: you love throws and want the Chinese flavor of them.

လက်ဝှေ့
Myanmar

Lethwei

“The art of nine limbs” — Myanmar's bare-knuckle ring sport, where headbutts are legal, points are a foreign concept, and fights end by knockout or draw.

FactDetail
OriginMyanmar, from centuries of village festival fighting into modern stadium rules
The nine limbsFists, elbows, knees, feet and shins — and the head
TraditionFighters once wrapped hands in hemp; wins came by knockout, with draws honored rather than decided
Modern eraGloves and weight classes arrived recently; the challenge remains absolute
Famous namesDave Leduc, the Canadian who became lethwei's most visible modern champion

A typical class

  1. Skip rope and conditioning (15 min)
  2. Striking rounds — all nine weapons (20 min)
  3. Clinch and neck work (10 min)
  4. Sparring for the prepared

Known for

The most complete striking ruleset on earth — every natural weapon legal — and a culture that treats a draw with respect.

Start here if: you're already an experienced striker looking for the deep end.

極真会
Japan · 1964

Kyokushin

“The ultimate truth” — Mas Oyama's full-contact karate, where knockdown tournaments replaced point sparring and conditioning became a philosophy.

FactDetail
OriginJapan, founded by Mas Oyama in 1964 after his mountain training and famous bull fights
RulesKnockdown sparring — full power, no punches to the face, kicks everywhere else
TrialsThe 100-man kumite — a hundred bouts in one day, first completed by Oyama himself
Core trainingHard kihon · Kata · Hojo undo conditioning · Knockdown sparring
Famous namesMas Oyama, Steve Arneil (the first to repeat the 100-man fight), Shokei Matsui

A typical class

  1. Strenuous warm-up and knuckle conditioning (15 min)
  2. Kihon — full power, full count (20 min)
  3. Kata (10 min)
  4. Fighting: lines, drills, then knockdown sparring (15 min)

Known for

The strongest karate brand on the planet — kata discipline married to genuine contact. Its offshoots (Ashihara, Enshin, and via its students much of Dutch kickboxing) changed global fighting.

Start here if: you want contact and tradition in one syllabus.

Jeet Kune Do
USA · 1967

Jeet Kune Do

Bruce Lee's “way of the intercepting fist” — less a style than a research program: keep what works, discard what doesn't, and hit the opponent on their way in.

FactDetail
OriginSeattle, Oakland, Los Angeles — developed by Bruce Lee through the 1960s and named in 1967
IngredientsWing Chun structure · Fencing-derived footwork and interception · Boxing punches · Grappling awareness
Signature ideasStrong side forward · Non-telegraphed strikes · “Using no way as way” — adaptability over style
LineageDan Inosanto, Lee's training partner, carries the art's teaching line today
LegacyThe philosophical grandfather of mixed martial arts

A typical class

  1. Footwork and the on-guard stance (15 min)
  2. Interception drills — the stop-hit (15 min)
  3. Trapping and combination work (15 min)
  4. Attribute training: speed, timing, distance

Known for

The idea that changed everything: train the fighter, not the style. Its five ways of attack are still the best vocabulary for describing how combinations happen.

Start here if: you think in concepts and want your training to be a research project.

武術
China · modern sport

Modern Wushu

China's performance martial art — traditional forms reorganized into a sport of explosive acrobatics, judged on difficulty, execution, and spectacle.

FactDetail
OriginThe People's Republic of China standardized it from the 1950s onward; its sister sport, sanda, covers the fighting side
EventsChangquan (long fist), nanquan (southern fist), taijiquan — plus daoshu, jianshu, gunshu, qiangshu weapon events
Core trainingFlexibility (extraordinary amounts) · Basics and jump techniques · Compulsory and individual routines
ScoringDifficulty values plus execution quality, like gymnastics with blades
Famous namesJet Li, a five-time all-China wushu champion before he was a movie star

A typical class

  1. Deep stretching — splits are homework (20 min)
  2. Basics: stances, strikes, jump techniques (20 min)
  3. Routine practice in sections (15 min)
  4. Conditioning and strength finish

Known for

The highest athletic ceiling in the martial world — 540s, butterfly twists, aerials with weapons — and the sport that carried kung fu's imagery to every screen.

Start here if: performance is your fight.

少林功夫
China · Songshan

Shaolin Kung Fu

The most famous name in the martial world — a Chan Buddhist temple whose monks became China's legendary fighters, and whose modern schools train students from every continent.

FactDetail
OriginThe Shaolin temple on Mount Song; the Bodhidharma legend ties the arts to Chan Buddhism, though history is layered with myth
Legend & historyThe “thirteen monks who saved a Tang prince” made the temple's reputation; the temple was burned in 1928 and revived after the Cultural Revolution
Core trainingStances and stamina · Traditional forms · Weapons · Conditioning (including hard qigong demonstrations)
Modern practiceLarge academies around the temple train children through adults in year-round programs
Famous namesThe warrior monks themselves — and Jet Li, whose film Shaolin Temple (1982) reignited the world's interest

A typical class

  1. Long, deep stretching and stances (20 min)
  2. Basics and forms of the student's level (25 min)
  3. Weapons or paired applications (10 min)
  4. Conditioning — the unglamorous engine of it all

Known for

The deepest catalog of forms in existence and a brand five centuries deep. History and legend share the syllabus — visitors should enjoy both and check both.

Start here if: you want immersion: forms, discipline, and a temple morning bell.
