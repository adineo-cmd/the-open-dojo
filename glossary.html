Library / Glossary

A–Z glossary

Every term a student meets, from every art in the library, in one alphabetical shelf. One line each — when a word deserves a paragraph, the deeper sections link from it.

A

Age-uke 揚げ受け
Rising block against a head-height attack.
Aikido 合気道
“The way of harmonious energy” — Ueshiba's art of blending, locks, and pins.
Arnis / Kali / Eskrima
The Filipino stick, blade, and empty-hand arts under three names.
Ashi-waza 足技
Judo's leg and foot techniques — the sweeps and reaps.
Atemi 当て身
A strike to a vulnerable point — the attention-getter of the Japanese arts.

B

Bokken / bokutō 木刀
The wooden training sword.
Bong sau 膀手
Wing Chun's wing-arm deflection.
Bushidō 武士道
“The way of the warrior” — the samurai ethical code.
Butsukari-geiko
Sumo's charging drill against a partner or pole.

C

Capoeira
The Afro-Brazilian art of kicks, music, and the roda.
Chagi 차기
Korean for “kick.”
Charyeot 차렷
“Attention” — the Korean command.
Chi sau 黐手
Wing Chun's “sticky hands” sensitivity drill.
Chūdan 中段
Middle level — the torso as a target zone.

D

Dachi / tachi 立ち
Stance — the suffix of every karate stance name.
Dan 段
Black-belt grade, counting upward from shodan.
Dao 刀
The Chinese curved saber — “general of weapons.”
Dō 道
“Way” — a fighting method reframed as personal development.
Dobok 도복
The Korean training uniform.
Dohyō 土俵
The sumo ring.
Dōjō 道場
“Place of the way” — the training hall.
Dollyo chagi
Korean for roundhouse kick.

E–F

Empi / enpi 肘
The elbow — both a joint and an elbow strike.
Fouetté
Savate's whipping toe kick, delivered in shoes.

G

Gedan 下段
Lower level — below the belt.
Geri 蹴り
Kick — the suffix of every karate kick name.
Gi 着
Shorthand for the training uniform (properly keikogi).
Ginga
Capoeira's swaying foundation step.
Gong bu 弓步
“Bow stance” — the Chinese front stance.
Guard
Both the defensive hand position and the grappling position on your back.
Gyaku 逆
“Reverse” — as in gyaku-zuki, the rear-hand punch.

H

Hadaka-jime 裸絞め
The rear naked choke.
Hajime 始め
“Begin.”
Hakama 袴
The pleated wide trousers of aikido and kendo.
Hanmi 半身
The bladed, angled posture of the Japanese arts.
Hapkido 합기도
“Coordinated energy way” — Korea's locks-and-kicks hybrid.
Hara 腹
The belly — center of gravity and of calm.
HEMA
Historical European martial arts, rebuilt from the old manuals.
Hiza 膝
The knee.

I–J

Iaido 居合道
The art of drawing, cutting, and resheathing the sword.
Ippon 一本
“One point” — the full-value score that ends a judo or karate match.
Jigeiko 地稽古
Kendo's free practice.
Jō 杖
The four-foot staff.
Jōdan 上段
Upper level — the head.
Judo 柔道
“The gentle way” — Kano's art of throws and groundwork.
Jujutsu 柔術
The classical Japanese methods of close combat.

K

Kata 型
A solo form — the textbook of a striking art.
Kata-gatame 肩固め
The shoulder hold — pin and choke in one.
Keikogi 稽古着
The training uniform, “gi” for short.
Kendo 剣道
“The way of the sword” — bamboo-sword fencing.
Kiai / kihap 気合・기합
The spirit shout that joins breath, body, and intent.
Kiba-dachi 騎馬立ち
The horse-riding stance.
Kihon 基本
Basics — the drill-floor vocabulary of an art.
Kime 決め
The instant of total focus at a technique's end.
Kimarite 決まり手
Sumo's named winning techniques — 82 of them.
Kobudo 古武道
The classical weapons arts, especially Okinawa's.
Kotegaeshi 小手返し
The wrist turn-out throw of aikido.
Kumite 組手
Sparring — “joined hands.”
Kung fu 功夫
“Effort over time” — the Western name for Chinese martial arts.
Kuzushi 崩し
Off-balancing — every throw begins by breaking balance.
Kyū 級
Student grade, counting downward toward black belt.
Kwoon 館
The Chinese training hall.

L

Leitai 擂台
The raised fighting platform of Chinese challenge matches and sanda.
Lethwei
Myanmar's bare-knuckle art of nine limbs.

M

Ma-ai 間合い
Fighting distance — the interval both fighters negotiate.
Makiwara 巻藁
The struck pad of Okinawan karate, for conditioning straight punches.
Mawashi 廻し
The sumo belt — and the prefix “round/turning” in mawashi-geri.
Men 面
Kendo's helmet — and the head strike named for it.
Mokuso 黙想
“Silent reflection” — class's kneeling bookends.
Mount
Grappling's dominant position, seated on the opponent.
Mudansha 無段者
Those below dan rank — the student body.
Mushin 無心
“No mind” — action without hesitation.

N–O

Nage 投げ
The thrower — the partner who performs the technique.
Ne-waza 寝技
Judo's ground techniques.
Nunchaku ヌンチャク
Two staves joined by cord — train slowly.
Obi 帯
The belt, knotted ends even.
Osae-komi 押込
“Held down” — judo's pinning call.
Osu 押忍
The dojo's do-everything syllable in many schools.

P–Q

Pak sau 拍手
Wing Chun's slapping-hand redirect.
Poomsae 품새
WT taekwondo's name for forms.
Qi 氣
Breath and energy — the internal arts' central idea.

R

Randori 乱取り
Judo's free practice — the model for all live drilling.
Rei 礼
The bow — respect made physical.
Roda
Capoeira's circle of players and musicians.
Ryū 流
“School” or “current” — a lineage of technique.

S

Sanda 散打
Chinese kickboxing with throws, fought on the leitai.
Seiken 正拳
The basic two-knuckle fist.
Seiza 正座
The formal kneeling posture.
Sensei 先生
Teacher — “one born before.”
Shinai 竹刀
The bamboo practice sword of kendo.
Shodan 初段
First-degree black belt — the beginning.
Shime-waza 絞技
Judo's choking techniques.
Shuai jiao 摔跤
Chinese jacket wrestling.
Sifu 師傅
The Chinese teacher — “skilled father.”
Sinawali
The Filipino paired-stick weaving patterns.

T

Tachi-ai 立ち合い
Sumo's initial charge.
Tai chi (taijiquan) 太極拳
The internal Chinese art of structure and relaxation.
Taolu 套路
Chinese solo forms — kata's Chinese name.
Tatami 畳
The woven mats of the Japanese training hall.
Te 手
Hand — as in karate, “empty hand.”
Tenkan 転換
The turning movement of aikido's footwork.
Tameshiwari 試し割り
Breaking — boards and bricks as a demonstration of trained structure, not party tricks.
Tsuki 突き
Thrusting punch — the suffix of every straight punch name.

U

Uchi 打ち
Striking — the suffix of swinging strikes.
Uke 受け
Both “block” and the training partner who receives a technique.
Ukemi 受け身
Receiving — the art of falling safely.
Urumi
Kalaripayattu's flexible ribbon-sword — the last weapon taught.

V–W

Vovinam
Vietnam's hard-soft national art, founded 1938.
Waza 技
Technique — the suffix of every judo technique family.
Wushu 武術
“Martial arts” generally — and China's modern performance sport.

Y–Z

Yame 止め
“Stop” — freeze instantly.
Yokozuna 横綱
Sumo's grand champion rank.
Yudansha 有段者
Those holding dan rank — the black belts.
Zanshin 残心
“Remaining mind” — awareness that outlasts the technique.
Zenkutsu-dachi 前屈立ち
The front stance — karate's driving platform.

Missing a word? The deeper sections hold hundreds more with context — start at Terminology, or send the term and it joins the shelf.
